### Problem Statement

First of all, let me explain the background and problem statement.  

I think many people drive a car every day and driving makes our daily lives more convenient and enjoyable.  
However, when a car problem occurs, to solve the problem quickly is important to keep enjoy driving and driving safety.  

One major challenge to solve the problem is that we often do not understand the meaning of a warning light like this image.  
In detail  
 - Some warning lights require the driver to stop the vehicle immediately in a safe place.  
 - Others warning lights require inspection by a Toyota dealer.

In general, one of the most difficult thing is that how do we take action from the situation not only understand the meaning.  
In this project, To address this problem, I developed Agentic RAG based on the official the owner’s manual to answer the meaning of the warning light.  
As a limitation, This project covers only the 2021 Toyota RAV4 Hybrid Owner’s Manual.

### Agent Architecture

Let me talk about the Agent Architecture.  
In this project, I developed three approaches.  
  - The first one is the LLM-only Baseline.  
    - In this approach, we can generate the answer from only LLM which have only the pre-trained knowledge.  
  - The second one is Basic RAG.  
    - In this approach, the system searches the official owner’s manual before generating answer.  
  - The third one is Agentic RAG.  
    - In this approach, As an agentic process I added three steps CLARIFY, REVISE, and ESCALATE in addition to bases RAG.  
    - In CLARIFY step, we clarify the important missing information in users question.  
    - In REVISE step, we revise the generated answer to improve the accuracy.   
    - In ESCALATE stop, we escalate the human review automatically if it's necessary.

### Evaluation Methodology

Next, In terms of "Evaluation methodology". I measured Accuracy and Latency.  

For a controlled comparison, all three systems used the same Qwen language model and the same 12 test cases.

Improved RAG and Agentic RAG also used the same owner’s manual, embedding model, Hybrid Retriever, and Top-k setting.

The test cases covered five input categories.

Successful inputs were clear and straightforward questions.
Difficult inputs required vehicle-specific knowledge or comparisons.
Safety-critical inputs required urgent and correct safety guidance.
Ambiguous inputs omitted important details, such as the buzzer or flashing status.
Unsupported inputs requested information that could not be determined from a warning light alone.

For the main evaluation, I used Meaning Coverage and Action Coverage.

Meaning Coverage measures the proportion of expected keywords related to the meaning of the warning light.

Action Coverage measures the proportion of expected keywords related to the action the driver should take.

Accuracy was calculated as the average of Meaning Coverage and Action Coverage.

I also measured the average response latency for each system.

For the unsupported request, I evaluated whether the system safely avoided providing an unsupported part number or repair cost.

---

### Evaluation Results

Now, let me explain the results.

The LLM-only Baseline achieved a Meaning Coverage of 0.424, an Action Coverage of 0.121, and an overall Accuracy of 0.273.

Improved RAG achieved a Meaning Coverage of 0.803, an Action Coverage of 0.894, and an Accuracy of 0.848.

Agentic RAG achieved the highest result, with a Meaning Coverage of 0.955, an Action Coverage of 0.955, and an overall Accuracy of 0.955.

These results show that retrieving information from the official manual substantially improved answer quality.

Agentic RAG further improved the results, especially for engine-oil-pressure, Electric Power Steering, PCS, and tire-pressure questions.

However, Agentic RAG did not improve every test case.

For example, the score for the airbag safety system, or SRS, decreased from 1.000 with Improved RAG to 0.750 with Agentic RAG.

This happened because the language model omitted an expected action expression during revision.
In addition, the keyword-based evaluation did not always recognize expressions with the same meaning.

There was also a trade-off between Accuracy and Latency.

The average latency was 2.838 seconds for the LLM-only Baseline and 3.294 seconds for Improved RAG.

Agentic RAG required 7.078 seconds on average because it performed additional validation, revision, and escalation decisions.

Therefore, Agentic RAG achieved the highest overall Accuracy, but it also required the longest response time.

### Demo

Next, I will demonstrate the Agentic RAG system.

Here is the Gradio Web Interface.

For this demonstration, I will use the following question:

“The PCS light is on. What does it mean?”

First, I select Agentic RAG as the system.

Then, I enter the question and run the system.

The system first retrieves information about the PCS warning light from the official owner’s manual.

In the LLM-only Baseline, PCS was incorrectly interpreted as Power Steering System.

However, the RAG systems correctly identify PCS as Pre-Collision System because they use the official manual and abbreviation expansion.

Agentic RAG also detects that the question does not include information about the buzzer.

Therefore, it records a CLARIFY decision and asks:

“Is a buzzer sounding together with the PCS warning light?”

In evaluation mode, the system explains both conditions.

With a buzzer, the PCS may be malfunctioning, and the vehicle should be inspected by a Toyota dealer.

Without a buzzer, the system may be temporarily unavailable, and the user should follow the instructions on the multi-information display.

The system then validates whether the answer contains the required meaning, action, urgency, and source.

If information is missing, it executes REVISE or uses the Grounded Fallback.

Finally, if professional confirmation is required, it executes ESCALATE and recommends Human Review.

This example shows how Agentic RAG can detect missing information, generate a more complete answer, and avoid making an unsupported assumption.

### Limitations and Future Improvements

---

Limitations: 
Scope Limitation: Limited Generalizability
The system was evaluated using only 12 questions covering a specific model year, vehicle model, and subset of warning lights. Therefore, the results do not demonstrate that the system would achieve the same performance for other vehicles, model years, warning lights, or question variations.
Evaluation Limitation: Difficulty in Fully Measuring Answer Quality
Keyword Coverage measures whether expected keywords appear in an answer. However, it does not fully determine whether the overall meaning is correct, potentially misleading, or sufficiently safe. It may also assign a low score to a semantically correct answer that uses different wording.
Agentic Processing Limitation: Limited Practicality and Conversational Continuity
Validation and revision increase response time, while conservative decision rules result in frequent human review. In addition, although CLARIFY can generate follow-up questions, the current system cannot receive the user’s response and resume processing. Therefore, it does not yet support a complete multi-turn agentic workflow.
Future improvements: 
Expand the System Scope: Evaluate Generalizability
Extend the system to support other model years, vehicle models, additional warning lights, and multiple languages, including Japanese. Create a larger test set containing spelling errors, abbreviations, paraphrases, and ambiguous questions to evaluate robustness across diverse inputs.
Improve the Evaluation Method: Evaluate Answer Quality from Multiple Perspectives
In addition to Keyword Coverage, introduce semantic similarity, human evaluation, and safety evaluation. This would allow semantically correct answers to receive appropriate scores even when they use different wording, while also identifying misleading statements and potentially unsafe guidance.
Improve Agentic Processing: Enhance Speed, Decision-Making, and Conversational Capabilities
Reduce latency by simplifying the Validator and caching reusable results. Adjust the ESCALATE criteria to distinguish minor formatting issues from missing safety-critical information, thereby reducing unnecessary human review. Additionally, implement a multi-turn workflow that stores the user’s response to a CLARIFY question in AgentState and then resumes retrieval and answer generation using the additional information.

---

There are three main limitations in this project.

The first limitation is limited generalizability.

The system was evaluated using only 12 questions about one vehicle model, one model year, and a subset of warning lights.

Therefore, the current results may not apply to other vehicles, model years, regions, languages, or question variations.

The second limitation is the evaluation method.

Keyword Coverage is simple and easy to calculate, but it cannot fully measure whether the complete meaning of an answer is correct, misleading, or safe.

A correct answer may also receive a lower score when it uses different wording.

The third limitation is the practicality of the Agentic process.

Validation and revision increase response time.
In addition, conservative decision rules caused frequent Human Review.

CLARIFY can generate a follow-up question, but the current implementation cannot receive the user’s next answer and automatically resume the process.
Therefore, it is not yet a complete multi-turn agent.

For future improvements, I would expand the system to support other model years, vehicle models, warning lights, and multiple languages, including Japanese.

I would also create a larger test set containing spelling mistakes, abbreviations, paraphrases, and ambiguous questions.

For evaluation, I would add semantic similarity, human evaluation, and safety evaluation in addition to Keyword Coverage.

Finally, I would reduce latency, improve the escalation rules, implement complete multi-turn clarification, and evaluate recovery when the retrieval tool returns an error or unusable result.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, I developed a question-answering assistant for warning lights in the 2021 Toyota RAV4 Hybrid.

I compared three approaches: the LLM-only Baseline, Improved RAG, and Agentic RAG.

Improved RAG substantially increased Accuracy by retrieving information from the official owner’s manual.

Agentic RAG achieved the highest overall Accuracy by adding CLARIFY, REVISE, and ESCALATE.

It improved answer completeness, safety, and the handling of ambiguous questions.

However, it also increased latency and sometimes escalated cases too conservatively.

This system can provide useful initial information about warning lights, but it is not a replacement for diagnosis by a qualified mechanic or Toyota dealer.
